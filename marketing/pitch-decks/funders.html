The problem

Exams still run on paper, manual marking, and hope for early grades.

Every term, schools rebuild the same fragile process from scratch: creating, reviewing, printing, securing, and storing paper scripts, tracking results by hand, and trusting that integrity is upheld.

Paper, start to finish
Creating, reviewing, printing, securing, and storing scripts by hand. Every step is slow and easy to get wrong.
Priced for markets that are not the Caribbean
International platforms charge in the neighborhood of $25 per student. Most Jamaican schools do not need to budget for that.
Online tools with no real integrity tooling
Most online test tools do little to stop a student from switching tabs, pasting an answer, or signing in from two devices at once.
No built-in accommodation
Students who need extra support, like text to speech, are often left to manual, inconsistent workarounds.
The solution

One platform. Every kind of assessment. Priced for the market it actually serves.

From a five minute pop quiz to a full formal end of year exam, Smart Assess Ja carries every assessment a school gives, on one secure, affordable platform, built specifically for the Caribbean.

One digital record, start to finish
Every exam created, reviewed, delivered, and stored in one system. No printing, no lost scripts.
Priced for the region it serves
A fraction of the cost of comparable international platforms, structured to get cheaper per student as a school grows.
Integrity tooling built in
Fullscreen lock, tab-switch detection, single-device login, and kiosk mode. Included, not an add-on.
Accessible by design
A text-to-speech accommodation any teacher can turn on, no separate software or extra spend.

Integrity and trust, the actual moat

The tooling a generic quiz app does not have.

Fullscreen & tab-switch detection
The exam knows the moment a student leaves the window.
Single-device login
Signing in elsewhere signs out the other session.
Desktop kiosk mode
Detects an attempt to switch away even where the OS itself cannot fully prevent it.
Row-level security
Access rules are enforced by the database itself, not just application code.
Why this matters to the business Integrity and data protection are the hardest parts of this product to copy well, and the reason a school trusts a platform with a real exam in the first place. It is also aligned with Jamaica's Data Protection Act, and each school's data is isolated in its own environment.
Artificial intelligence, used responsibly and cheaply

A real product differentiator, run on a disciplined budget.

Teachers use AI to polish question wording and import questions straight from a PDF exam paper. A live help assistant answers questions for visitors and logged-in users. Every suggestion is reviewed by a human before it is used. AI never grades a student and never makes an integrity decision on its own.

Market opportunity

A real, recurring market, still open for a purpose-built player.

Jamaica's schools sit inside a strong existing exam culture, anchored by the region's own CSEC and CAPE examinations, so the habit of formal assessment is already there. What is missing is affordable, purpose-built software behind it. International platforms like exam.net price around $25 per student, well outside what most local schools can budget.

~470
Secondary schools in Jamaica, the initial addressable market
~205,000
Estimated secondary students nationwide
On the closest Jamaican player EduFocal (JSE-listed) is the other notable Jamaica-built edtech company, but it is built around a different job: EduFocal Academy is a self-directed, consumer-paid PEP exam-prep product for grades 4 to 6, not a school-administered secondary exam platform. Its newer Quizzative tool lets educators build online quizzes and reaches into secondary schools, making it the closest one to watch, but it is not built around the formal, integrity-monitored, school-wide exam delivery Smart Assess Ja is. It is also a company retrenching, not scaling: 2025 revenue fell 28% year over year, then a further 53% in Q1 2026, against a shareholders' equity deficit and roughly J$104,000 in cash at last report, alongside a public rebrand and an expanded mandate into unrelated industries outside education.

The one true marginal cost Real infrastructure cost per school runs roughly $330 to $390 per year today, driven almost entirely by one line item (a per-school database), and converges toward roughly $325 as more schools come on. AI spend is a rounding error against that. This is a business that gets structurally more profitable per customer as it scales, not less.
